<section id="approach">
  <p class="eyebrow">{'02 / Analytical approach'}</p>
  <h2>Build the reporting base, then preserve the exceptions</h2>
  <p>
    {
      'I developed the Athena SQL through successive review and validation cycles. I established the purchase and posting base, added customer and classification context, and aggregated clicks and applications before connecting them to the reporting rows.'
    }
  </p>
  <ol>
    <li>
      <strong>Define and test each reporting grain.</strong> I identified what purchase,
      line-item, posting, customer, click, and application records represented before
      combining them. This made the risk of omitted or repeated activity explicit.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Control relationships before joining.</strong> I tested how identifiers
      connected across sources, applied relevant date checks, and kept unmatched records
      available for investigation. A particular correction for incomplete relationship
      coverage was collaborative; I traced and validated the affected examples and
      incorporated the agreed treatment.
    </li>
    <li>
      <strong>Preserve one-sided engagement.</strong> I aggregated engagement before
      joining it to lower-level reporting rows and preserved activity when only clicks or
      only applications were present. This supported separate checks for missing,
      unmatched, and unexpectedly multiplied activity.
    </li>
  </ol>
  <p>
    {
      'I translated confirmed definitions into documented classifications and outputs. When the data did not determine the intended business meaning, I prepared examples and kept the question open for the responsible stakeholders rather than encoding an unsupported assumption.'
    }
  </p>
</section>

  <h2>Trace records and reconcile the whole output</h2>
  <p>
    {
      'I owned validation for the rebuilt dataset. I traced selected records from source to output, compared successive query results, reconciled aggregate totals, examined unmatched and unexpectedly repeated activity, and investigated exceptions across Athena, SQL Server, and internal reporting references.'
    }
  </p>
  <p>
    {
      'I also compared the dataset with Finance reporting across several KPI families. I documented where results aligned, where they differed, and which definition or adjustment questions required further review. Possible explanations remained review items unless the responsible stakeholders confirmed them.'
