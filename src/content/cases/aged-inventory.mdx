  <section id="approach">
    <p class="eyebrow">02 / Analytical approach</p>
    <h2>Build a traceable path from source inputs to business review</h2>
    <h3>Define and connect the decision population</h3>
    <p>
      {
        'I tested which sales population was appropriate for an FBA aged-inventory review and used a filtered FBA view for the model. Identifier-based matching connected each modeled item to the product, inventory, sales, cost, fee, classification, size, weight, and historical-price information required by the scenario calculations.'
      }
    </p>
    <h3>Normalize inputs before applying business logic</h3>
    <p>
      {
        'I standardized relevant product classifications and measurement units and used size and weight inputs for fee calculations. Missing or unrecognized values remained visible rather than being silently assigned a result.'
      }
    </p>
    <h3>Measure movement and possible future exposure</h3>
    <p>
      {
        'Recent FBA sales were aggregated to estimate sales velocity and inventory coverage. The sell-through path used a documented working movement assumption to project monthly units, time to clear, inventory runoff, and possible future aged-inventory fee exposure. The discount assumption changed price-dependent economics; the separate movement assumption estimated sell-through. This was assumption-based scenario analysis, not a demand forecast, causal price response, or formal price-elasticity model.'
      }
    </p>
    <h3>Model three separate financial paths</h3>
    <p>
      {
        'I built separate financial paths for removal or disposal, discounted sell-through, and liquidation. The removal path combined direct removal cost with relevant unrecovered costs. The sell-through path recalculated selected unit economics at a discounted price and projected the financial effect of clearing the aged quantity over time. The liquidation path estimated recovery after applicable deductions and relevant unrecovered costs.'
      }
    </p>
    <h3>Compare the result and explain the recommendation</h3>
    <p>
      {
        'The workbook compared all three outcomes and selected the strongest financial result. If every option was negative, it selected the least-negative modeled result rather than presenting an unprofitable path as a gain. It then translated the result into a proposed action and a plain-English explanation.'
      }
    </p>

    <p class="eyebrow">04 / Validation and review</p>
    <h2>Hold incomplete records and unsettled meanings for review</h2>
    <p>
      {
        'Recommendation logic was gated by checks for required inputs, usable classifications, available fee outputs, and complete scenario results. Rows with missing or unsuitable prerequisites were held for review instead of receiving an unexplained action. These controls made important gaps visible; they did not independently guarantee that every upstream value or business assumption was correct.'
      }
    </p>
    <p>
      {
        'I documented actual formula behavior and kept unresolved interpretations visible. This separated what the workbook calculated from business meanings that still required responsible-owner review.'
